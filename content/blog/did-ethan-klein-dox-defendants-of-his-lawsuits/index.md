Doxing Claims Related to Lawsuits

## Background

* On June 19, 2025, Ethan Klein announced lawsuits against Twitch creators Denims, Kaceytron, and Frogan for copyright infringement on the h3h3Productions channel.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup><sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup><sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
* The first page of a civil lawsuit typically includes the parties' full legal names, court information, and case number.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
* Moderators of a snark subreddit are being sued for contributory copyright infringement as "Does" since their identities are not known.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>

## Claim

Ethan Klein doxed people by:
1. Showing the legal names of Denims, Frogan, and Kaceytron when displaying the lawsuit in a video.
2. Seeking to reveal the identities of snark moderators being sued for contributory copyright infringement.

## Truth

* **Public Court Records:** Court filings are generally public in the United States and accessible online via the Public Access to Court Electronic Records (PACER) system.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> The information displayed by Ethan was already part of the public legal record.
* **Standard Legal Procedure:** In "John Doe" lawsuits, the next step is to determine defendants' identities, amend the complaint to replace "Doe" with true names, and serve the lawsuits. The judge has granted subpoenas to platforms like Reddit to release information about the moderators, which is standard legal procedure.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup><sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
* **YouTube Policy Compliance:** YouTube's privacy policy allows reporting if someone posts your personal information. The announcement video remains up and has not been removed for policy violation.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>

## Conclusion

Ethan Klein did not dox individuals by announcing lawsuits. He used publicly available court information to discuss legal proceedings. Identifying defendants in ongoing litigation through proper legal channels is standard procedure, not malicious exposure of private information.
