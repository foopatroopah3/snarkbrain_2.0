## Background

* In Nazi concentration camps, kapos were prisoners assigned to supervise other prisoners, often on forced labor details. It was a specific role within the camp system.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* Jewish Voice for Peace (JVP) is a U.S. grassroots Jewish organization that organizes Jews in solidarity with the Palestinian freedom struggle.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
* The slogan “from the river to the sea, Palestine will be free” refers to the land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea, now Israel and the Palestinian territories. Some Palestinian activists use it to call for freedom and equal rights across that area. Earlier Arabic variants said “from the water to the water, Palestine is Arab”; an Islamist version said “from the water to the water, Palestine is Islamic.” The familiar English wording “from the river to the sea, Palestine will be free” is a distinct version. The phrase has also appeared in militant rhetoric: Hamas’s 2017 charter rejects any alternative to the “full and complete liberation” of Palestine from river to sea, and Palestinian Islamic Jihad has described the land as Arab Islamic land and Israel’s presence there as illegitimate. In 1966, Syrian leader Hafez al Assad threatened to “throw you, aggressors, into the sea.” Against that background, many Jewish and Israeli listeners hear the slogan as a call to replace Israel with a single Palestinian state, leaving the future status of Jews unclear at best.<sup>[[3]](#ref3) [[4]](#ref4)</sup>
* About a month after Hamas’s October 7 attacks on Israel, Ethan Klein joined Hasan Piker’s livestream on November 9, 2023, for a friendly conversation about Israel and Palestine.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> The slogan came up in their discussion. Ethan said he was not personally offended and did not want people to stop using the slogan.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> He believed the vast majority of people who use it did not want to kill Jews.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup> His frustration was that people on the left seemed indifferent to Jewish concerns, arguing with Jews who described discomfort instead of hearing them.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Claim

Ethan Klein compared Jewish Voice for Peace to kapos.

## Truth

* **Hasan treated JVP’s use of the slogan as evidence against Jewish objections to it.** He offered the organization as a Jewish counterexample to Ethan’s point that many Jews hear the phrase as threatening. Ethan objected that one group’s position could not establish how the wider Jewish community feels.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
* **Ethan used kapos as an extreme example of how widely Jewish views can differ.** His point was that finding Jewish people who defend even an extreme position does not show that other Jews are comfortable with it. He was illustrating the weakness of using selected people to speak for a whole community, not equating JVP members with collaborators.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
* **Ethan corrected Hasan and explained what his extreme example was meant to show.** When Hasan said he understood Ethan to have compared JVP with kapos, Ethan rejected that interpretation. He clarified that almost any position can find a defender. The same point applied to JVP: its support for the slogan shows that Jewish opinion varies, but it cannot establish how Jews generally feel or invalidate the fear some express. Treating JVP as the Jewish voice that decides whether other Jews are entitled to feel threatened would tokenize its members and make an invalid generalization about the community.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>

## Conclusion

Ethan’s kapos example illustrated the range of Jewish opinion. He did not compare JVP to kapos. When Hasan said he had, Ethan corrected him and explained that citing a few Jewish voices cannot determine what a whole community feels.
