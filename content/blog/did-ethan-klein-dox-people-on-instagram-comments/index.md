## Background

* On February 28, 2025, during H3 Show #116, Hila Klein discussed her new Star of David tattoo, which she had posted on Instagram. The post received hate comments that were discussed during the episode.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* Ethan reviewed some of these commenters' public Instagram profiles during the podcast segment.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>

## Claim

Ethan Klein doxed detractors on Instagram.

## Truth

* **What is Doxing?** Doxing involves exposing private or identifying information without consent, usually with malicious intent. Examples include revealing real names of anonymous people, addresses, phone numbers, or workplaces.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
* **First Comment Analysis:** One comment read "Easily identifiable," a reference to the WWII era, when Nazis required Jews to wear distinctive markings.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup> Ethan viewed the commenter's profile, found it was private, and discovered the profile picture was of H3 employee Lena Ayad. The account was reported.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
* **Second Comment Analysis:** Another comment read "Glad that's behind me. Enjoy hell." Ethan viewed the commenter's public photos<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup> and wondered aloud whether the friends and family in them knew about his racist remarks.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
* **YouTube Policy:** YouTube allows users to request removal of content containing personal information, but the video segment remains up without policy violations.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>

## Conclusion

Ethan Klein did not dox individuals by reviewing Instagram comments. He examined publicly available information from profiles that had posted hateful comments on Hila's post. No private addresses, phone numbers, workplaces, or other confidential information was revealed. The discussion centered on publicly posted content and its implications.
