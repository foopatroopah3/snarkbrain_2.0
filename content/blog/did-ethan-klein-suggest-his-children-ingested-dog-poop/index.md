## Background

* Giardiasis, caused by the protist Giardia duodenalis, is one of the most common parasitic diseases in humans.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* On February 17th, 2025, Ethan mentioned during the H3 Podcast that the family's newly adopted dog, Olive, had been diagnosed with a Giardia infection.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
* Ethan speculated that symptoms his infant son Sunny had been experiencing might be related to contracting the infection from Olive, noting that giardiasis spreads through the fecal-oral route via contaminated surfaces or insufficient hand washing.
* On March 1st, 2025, content creators BadEmpanada and Denims spread the narrative that Ethan had described his children eating dog feces in their coverage of the situation.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
* On March 7th, 2025, Child Protective Services visited the Klein home after receiving an anonymous call claiming the Kleins' children were "crawling around eating dog poop and getting viruses."<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>

## Claims

* Ethan Klein stated or implied that his children were directly ingesting dog feces.
* The children were infected with Giardia from the family dog.
* The situation represented such severe neglect and risk that it warranted a CPS investigation.

## Truth

* **No Giardia Diagnosis:** Ethan explicitly stated that neither he, Hila, nor Sunny had been diagnosed with giardiasis. They had taken Sunny for testing, but no one in the household other than the dog, Olive, ever contracted the infection.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup><sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
* **No Suggestion of Direct Contact:** Ethan never stated or implied his children were touching or eating dog feces. The false narrative was created by deceptively combining two separate statements:
  * Statement A: "Sunny's crawling around, he's putting everything in his mouth, he's at that age"<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
  * Statement B: "It's extremely ridiculous to get... you have to ingest my poop"<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
* **Context:** Statement B was made in response to an employee asking about transmission in the office setting, where no children or dogs were present. Ethan clarified that transmission between adults in an office would require unusual circumstances, while transmission between a pet and infant could occur through normal household contamination of surfaces.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
* **Comedic Exaggeration:** As a comedy podcaster, Ethan frequently uses hyperbolic and exaggerated language for humor. Statement B was clearly meant as comedic exaggeration when explaining disease transmission, not a literal description of events in his household.
* **Common Condition:** Giardiasis is not rare or particularly serious when treated. Infection rates reach up to 7% in developed countries, and risk factors include childcare settings and contact with infected animals.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* **No Case Opened:** Child Protective Services investigated the claims and determined they were unfounded, closing the matter without opening a formal case.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>

## Conclusion

The claim that Ethan Klein suggested his children were eating dog feces is a complete fabrication created by taking his comedic exaggerations out of context. While Ethan discussed the possibility of giardiasis transmission through normal household contamination—a common concern for pet owners—he never described unsanitary conditions or direct contact with feces. The CPS visit was prompted by a malicious false report based on distorted information spread online, and authorities determined the allegations were unfounded.
