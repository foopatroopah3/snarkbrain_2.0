## Background

* In 2016, Ethan Klein was sued by Matt Hosseinzadeh for copyright infringement.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* Philip DeFranco launched a GoFundMe to help fund Ethan and Hila in defense of the lawsuit. It raised $170k.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
  
## Claim

Ethan committed some sort of financial impropriety with the FUPA Fund.

## Truth

* The express purpose of Philip DeFranco's GoFundMe was: "Please join me in donating to H3H3's defense fund and taking a stand together as a community. If you don't have the cash, please just get the word out about this."<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup> So, when the campaign launched, there wasn't even a concept of the FUPA Fund, and most of the money was raised before it was announced. It was intended solely for the Kleins.
* The GoFundMe page was launched on the 25th of May whereas FUPA was announced on the 27th. By the time FUPA was announced, the campaign had already raised $117k, well surpassing its goal of $100k.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>
* Their attorney's original estimate was that the legal defense would cost $100k.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
* Since they raised much more, the Kleins generously came up with the idea of the FUPA Fund to give money back to the community after their lawsuit, saying that the money raised would become a community fund with them "...*as the first clients*".<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup>
* The fund was set up on the advice of their original attorney, who turned out to be wrong that such a nonprofit could legally be used to support the Kleins at all.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
* The lawsuit ended with a ruling that H3's reaction was "quintessential fair use," setting a precedent for reaction content and protecting future creators from legal threats over fair use commentary.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
* The legal defense, however, ended up costing well over $170k, with the first month of fees alone totaling over $56k. This was well past their original lawyer's estimate and left Ethan and Hila paying over $15k out of their own pockets.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup>
* Although the FUPA Fund did not materialize, Ethan and Hila continue to advocate for small creators against abuses of fair use. The House of Tabula, for example, praised Ethan and Hila for helping them with a predatory lawsuit.<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup>
* There is **not a single** reputable news source that alleges any financial impropriety by the Kleins.
* [This in-depth, 41-minute video delves deeper into the topic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B4YLocxPd10)

## Conclusion

All money raised for the Kleins' legal defense was raised with the understanding that it would be used only for the Kleins. The Kleins were misinformed about how much their legal defense would cost and, in a gesture of goodwill, said they would give excess funds to the community. The lawsuit ended up costing far more than the funds raised and the logistics of setting up a public legal fund were intractable. For these reasons, they gave up on the idea of the FUPA Fund.
