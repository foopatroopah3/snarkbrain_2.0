## Background

* Hila Klein launched Teddy Fresh in 2017, aiming to add diversity and color to the streetwear market. She drew inspiration from the playful nature of kids' clothing, vintage pieces, and high-quality fabrics.
* In 2021, Teddy Fresh created the "Gone but not forgotten" dinosaur sweater. The sweater was first teased during their annual "You Laugh, You Lose" episode on November 25, 2021.<sup>[[1]](#ref1)</sup>
* A now-deleted Reddit post by user u/amberlouiseb pointed out similarities between the dinosaur sweater designs and a Gary Kennedy knitting patterns book.<sup>[[2]](#ref2)</sup>
* This post led to broader accusations of copied work in other Teddy Fresh products.<sup>[[3]](#ref3)</sup>

## Claim

Hila Klein is knowingly stealing designs for her Teddy Fresh brand.

## Truth

* **Standard Industry Practice: Licensing:** Designers commonly license graphics for use in commercial collections. This legal process involves negotiating royalties, usage rights, and protection. Once finalized, the brand has the legal right to use the artwork.<sup>[[4]](#ref4)</sup>
* **The Gary Kennedy Dino Sweater – A Case of Internal Error:** Following the Reddit post, Ethan Klein addressed the situation publicly. He explained that the designs for the Styracosaurus and Stegosaurus were submitted by a former employee who presented them as their own original work.<sup>[[5]](#ref5)</sup> Upon discovering the error, Teddy Fresh contacted Gary Kennedy, explained the situation, and reached a licensing agreement. Kennedy agreed to the deal and has since continued to work with Teddy Fresh, allowing them to use more of his knitting designs.<sup>[[6]](#ref6)</sup>
* **Standard Industry Practice: Printed Fabric:** Clothing brands frequently purchase pre-printed fabric in bulk from textile wholesalers. These wholesalers specialize in selling designer fabrics for commercial use.<sup>[[7]](#ref7)</sup>
* **Rick and Morty Collaboration:** Teddy Fresh collaborated with the franchise *Rick and Morty* in 2022, with 2 of the 12 designs called out as similar to other brands' work.<sup>[[8]](#ref8)</sup> However, competitor Puma also released a *Rick and Morty* collaboration that same year,<sup>[[9]](#ref9)</sup> and the product development cycle for apparel typically takes close to a year from design to sale, making direct copying from another brand impractical.
* **Pinterest and Inspiration:** Critics have attempted to link design ideas to Pinterest boards. This platform is specifically designed for visual discovery, ideation, and finding inspiration.<sup>[[10]](#ref10)</sup>
* **The Unexplained Prada Design:** One Prada-style sweater remains without a clear explanation, and Ethan acknowledged it "probably" warrants a deeper look.<sup>[[11]](#ref11)</sup>
* **The Ratio:** In 2021, Teddy Fresh released over 1,000 products, yet fewer than 10 were accused of being copied. This ratio points to isolated incidents rather than a systemic issue.

## Conclusion

Public accusations of copying do not support the claim that Hila Klein is knowingly stealing designs. The dino sweater controversy stemmed from an internal submission error that was promptly rectified through transparency and a licensing deal with Gary Kennedy. Most other claims are rooted in standard industry practices like fabric sourcing or come from a *Rick and Morty* collaboration with design overlaps that were likely coincidental due to long production timelines. The single unaddressed design suggests oversight, not intentional theft. The company's corrective actions and ongoing collaborations with artists demonstrate a lack of solid evidence for intentional wrongdoing.
